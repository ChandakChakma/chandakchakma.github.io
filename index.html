Chandak Chakma

I am a final-year undergraduate in Robotics and Mechatronics Engineering at the University of Dhaka, graduating in October 2026. I am a research assistant at the Cortex AI Lab, University of Dhaka, and I did my undergraduate thesis in the Robotics Lab, advised by Dr. Sejuti Rahman.

My research asks one question: when a model draws many samples, does the spread of those samples tell the truth about what the model knows? I study this in two places. In video world models, I test whether the sampled futures match the real spread of outcomes, and how to fix them when they do not. In reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning (RLVR), I study how difficulty is measured, reward design beyond ground-truth labels, and the pass@1 versus pass@k trade-off.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

Only an Oracle Knows the Best Sample: Predictive Distributions in a One-Pass Vision World Model
Chandak Chakma
Preprint, 2026. Under review.
arXiv (coming soon)  /  project page (coming soon)

One-pass video world models such as DeltaWorld generate many futures in one forward pass and are trained and judged by the best of K samples. We show that this metric can prefer the worse-calibrated model: the samples form a tight core with far outliers, and the true future falls outside the samples too often. A controlled loss swap traces the problem to best-of-many training and reveals a ranking reversal between best-of-K and proper scores. ALE-WI, a short fine-tuning with a per-patch energy score and weight interpolation, improves CRPS by 2.1 to 2.8% on Kinetics, VSPW and KITTI, with the same architecture and sampling cost.

Unlearnable, or Unmeasured? On the Reliability of Difficulty Labels in RLVR
Chandak Chakma, Syed Nazmus Sakib, Nafiul Haque, Shifat E. Arman
NeurIPS 2026 Workshop: Transitioning from Pre-Training to Post-Training (Pre→Post)
arXiv  /  pdf

Recent RLVR work reports sets of "unlearnable" prompts that the policy never learns. We show that these sets depend heavily on how difficulty is measured: the same data gives 74 to 228 prompts under different aggregation rules, and re-evaluating a single checkpoint reproduces much of the disagreement across seeds. The slow-learning effect itself survives: these prompts learn at about one third of the normal rate. A binomial sampling model gives the number of rollouts a reproducible difficulty label needs, and a matched-sample check shrinks the reported gradient-similarity gap.

Escape Without a Recipe: Maneuver-Agnostic Mars Rover Recovery from Granular Entrapment
Meraj Hossain Promit, Chandak Chakma, Md Jubair Ahmed Sourov, Sejuti Rahman
IEEE IROS 2026 Workshop (SRW), Pittsburgh. Poster.
pdf

A recurrent policy learns to free a wheeled rover from loose regolith without a scripted maneuver. In a material point method (MPM) simulator, the policy escapes 62.5% of verified traps on average across five training seeds, against 7 of 32 for the best of ten scripted controllers. A numerical audit found a coupling-buffer fault that silently dropped 9.7% of contact nodes.
