Model Collapse and Weak-to-Strong Generalization

Date: November 6, 2026  |  Estimated Reading Time: 3 min  |  Author: Chandak Chakma

Training exclusively on self-generated data erodes the tails of the distribution, whereas labels from a weaker supervisor can still teach a stronger student. Two results that bound what self-generated labels can achieve.

19 Model collapse

Key resultrecursive training erodes the tails

Suppose each generation fits a Gaussian by maximum likelihood to \(N\) samples drawn from the previous generation’s fit. Since \(\mathbb{E}[\hat\sigma^2]=\frac{N-1}{N}\sigma^2\) for the maximum-likelihood variance estimate, iterating gives \[\mathbb{E}[\sigma_t^2]=\Big(\frac{N-1}{N}\Big)^{t}\sigma_0^2\ \longrightarrow\ 0 .\]

Examplethe rate of collapse

With \(N=100\) and \(t=100\), \((0.99)^{100}=0.366\): two thirds of the variance has been lost, and the rare cases (the tails) are the first to disappear.

Shumailov et al. (2024) demonstrate the same loss of tails in language models trained recursively on their own outputs. Gerstgrasser et al. (2024) show that accumulating real and synthetic data, rather than replacing earlier data, keeps the error bounded.

Why it matters

R-Zero’s Solver trains exclusively on labels it produced itself, which places it in the pure “replace” regime. The known remedies are retaining earlier data, anchoring to the base model, and incorporating external data.

20 Weak-to-strong generalization

Definitionperformance gap recovered (Burns et al., 2023)

\[\mathrm{PGR}=\frac{\mathrm{Perf}_{\text{weak}\to\text{strong}}-\mathrm{Perf}_{\text{weak}}}{\mathrm{Perf}_{\text{strong ceiling}}-\mathrm{Perf}_{\text{weak}}}.\]

Strong models trained on a weaker model’s labels often recover a substantial fraction of the gap to their ceiling performance: labels less accurate than the student can still teach it. This result is the optimistic counterpart to model collapse.

Why it matters

Noisy self-labels can still teach, provided that their errors are not ones the student already believes. Self-labels are risky precisely because their errors are, by construction, the student’s own beliefs.

Exercises

Exercise 11. In the Gaussian collapse model with \(N=50\), what fraction of the variance remains after \(50\) generations?

Show solution

\((49/50)^{50}=e^{50\ln0.98}=0.364\).
