Chandak Chakma

I am a Research Assistant at the Cortex AI Lab, where I work with Shifat E. Arman (now a DPhil student at Oxford) on reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning and on video world models. I recently completed my B.Sc. in Robotics and Mechatronics Engineering at the University of Dhaka (2026), where I did my undergraduate thesis in the Robotics Lab with Dr. Sejuti Rahman. I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027.

My research interests are self-evolving LLM reasoning, reinforcement learning for LLMs, and probabilistic evaluation of world models. A common thread in my work is whether the signals we use to train and evaluate models measure what we think they measure. My work has shown that which prompts count as “unlearnable” in RL with verifiable rewards depends heavily on how difficulty is measured (NeurIPS 2026 workshop), and that best-of-K, a common score for one-pass video world models, can rank the worse model first (under review at CVPR 2027). I have also co-authored work on planning with latent world models and on surgical video generation.

Currently, I study self-play for LLM reasoning, where a model writes its own problems and learns to solve them without human labels or an external verifier. I found that the usual reward cannot tell a hard question from an invalid one, and I am developing Valid-Zero, which checks each question before rewarding it. In the long term, I want to build models that keep learning to reason when no one gives them the answers.

I work on self-play and reinforcement learning for LLM reasoning, focusing on learning signals that need no answer key. I have also worked on predictive distributions in video world models.

Unlearnable, or Unmeasured? On the Reliability of Difficulty Labels in RLVR
Chandak Chakma, Syed Nazmus Sakib, Nafiul Haque, Shifat E. Arman
NeurIPS Workshop (Pre→Post), 2026
arXiv

Which RLVR prompts count as "unlearnable" depends on how difficulty is measured (the same data yields 74 to 228 of them), but the effect itself is real: these prompts learn about three times slower.

Only an Oracle Knows the Best Sample: Predictive Distributions in a One-Pass Vision World Model
Chandak Chakma
Preprint, 2026
project page / arXiv (coming soon)

Judging a one-pass video world model by its best-of-K sample rewards a tight core with far outliers; training every sample with a proper score instead (ALE-WI) gives futures that cover the truth, at the same architecture and sampling cost.

Escape Without a Recipe: Maneuver-Agnostic Mars Rover Recovery from Granular Entrapment
Meraj Hossain Promit, Chandak Chakma, Md Jubair Ahmed Sourov, Sejuti Rahman
IROS Space Robotics Workshop (SRW), 2026
pdf

A recurrent policy trained in a grain-level simulator frees a stuck rover without any scripted maneuver, escaping 62.5% of verified traps where the best of ten scripted controllers manages 7 of 32.

The RL Engine Behind LLM Reasoning

Every method for training LLM reasoners, from PPO and GRPO to self-play, estimates the same policy gradient. We derive it, show why a baseline introduces no bias, relate GRPO to RLOO, and examine what PPO clipping and the KL penalty actually control.

What RL Can and Cannot Do: Sharpening, pass@k and Entropy

The KL-regularized optimum rescales the odds of answers the base model already produces, which is why RL predominantly sharpens. We derive this result, identify its signature in pass@k curves, and discuss entropy collapse.

Self-Play as a Game: Cycling, Expert Iteration and STaR

Simultaneous gradient play can spiral or cycle; fictitious play, opponent pools and PSRO restore convergence. Expert iteration explains AlphaZero, and STaR turns out to be an EM procedure for the log-likelihood of the correct answer.
