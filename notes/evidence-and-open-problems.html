21 How big is the noise?

Key resultbenchmark uncertainty

On \(N\) problems with accuracy near \(p\), the standard error is \(\sqrt{p(1-p)/N}\) and a \(95\%\) interval is about \(\pm1.96\) standard errors.

benchmark problems \(N\) standard error at \(p=0.5\) \(95\%\) half-width
AIME (one year) \(30\) \(0.091\) \(\pm 17.9\) points
MATH-500 \(500\) \(0.022\) \(\pm 4.4\) points
GSM8K test \(1{,}319\) \(0.014\) \(\pm 2.7\) points

Averaging \(k\) samples per problem (avg@\(k\)) removes sampling noise, not the noise from which problems happen to be in the set. Compare two methods with a paired bootstrap over the same problems.

22 A checklist before you claim anything

  • Same compute: match rollouts, tokens and optimizer steps against every baseline.

  • Seeds: at least three for training curves; a “rise, then fall” claim needs the fall to exceed the seed spread.

  • pass@\(k\): the unbiased estimator with \(n\gg k\), reported up to large \(k\).

  • Newness: report base pass@256 and count problems the base model never solves.

  • Contamination: exact and \(13\)-gram overlap between generated problems and every benchmark.

  • Decision rules first: write the pass/fail rule before the run, and never pick checkpoints on test sets.

  • Read the code: published numbers come from code, not from equations (Section 10).

Open problems: where a new thread can lead

  1. Growth. Pure self-play peaks after about three to five rounds, and later Challengers do not teach more than earlier ones (What Actually Evolves in Self-Evolving RL?, ICLR 2027 submission). Collapse fixes such as R-Diverse and data gating (Survive or Collapse) stop the fall, but not the stall. Goal: a loop that keeps improving.

  2. Answer keys under shared errors. A label-free signal that beats the vote on the minority-truth zone (Section 17), using a channel that a shared misreading does not control (Sections 17.4 and 17.8).

  3. Search with a strict check. Find rare right answers and recognize them without labels. The check’s false-accept rate on the vote’s wrong answer must stay below the truth’s share of the samples (Section 18).

  4. Measured expansion. Newly solved problems at large \(k\), beyond the base model’s pass@256 (Section 7), instead of pass@1 gains that may be sharpening.

  5. Grounded teacher rewards without labels. SOAR shows that rewarding real student progress works, but it needs a labeled hard set (Section 10).

  6. A theory of closed-loop improvement. When can a loop with no outside information improve, and by how much? The data processing inequality (Section 15) and the verifier gap (Section 16) mark the boundary.
